Interest rates and the year ahead

The central bank raised its benchmark rate by a quarter point on Wednesday, the fourth increase this year, citing inflation that has stayed above its two percent target for eighteen months.

Officials signalled that further increases remain possible if price growth does not cool, though they stopped short of promising another move at the next meeting in December.

Markets reacted modestly. The broad index closed down 0.3 percent, while the two-year yield rose to 4.7 percent, its highest level since March.

Economists surveyed before the decision expected one more increase before the end of the year, but several noted that a weakening labour market could change that path.

Hiring slowed for a third month, and the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.1 percent. Wage growth, which the bank watches closely, eased to 3.9 percent from 4.2 percent.

The bank's chair said the committee is prepared to hold rates steady if the data cooperates, adding that policy is already restrictive enough to bring inflation down over time.

Consumer prices rose 2.6 percent over the past twelve months, down from a peak of 6.4 percent two years ago but still above the target the bank has set for itself.

Investors now price in a first cut in the second quarter of next year, later than they expected a month ago, according to futures markets.
