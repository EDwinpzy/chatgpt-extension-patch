Battery prices fall again

Cell prices dropped nine percent in the third quarter, the steepest decline in two years, as new capacity in Asia came online faster than demand grew.

Analysts expect the trend to continue into next year, which would make mid-range electric cars cheaper without any change to their batteries.

Raw material costs told the same story. Lithium carbonate, the benchmark input, trades at less than a fifth of its 2022 peak after a wave of new mines opened.

Manufacturers have responded by cutting prices rather than expanding margins, a pattern that has frustrated investors in the sector for three consecutive quarters.

Grid storage is the fastest growing buyer of the cheaper cells, with installations expected to double again next year according to industry forecasts.

Recycling remains the missing piece: less than six percent of retired packs are processed for their metals today, though several plants are under construction.
